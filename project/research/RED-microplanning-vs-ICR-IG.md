# RED Microplanning (WHO) vs ICR FHIR IG — Alignment & Gap Analysis

This analyzes WHO's *Microplanning for immunization service delivery using the Reaching Every District (RED) strategy* (WHO, ordering code WHO/IVB/09.11, printed October 2009; 74 pp. of content + back matter; Expanded Programme on Immunization, Dept. of Immunization, Vaccines and Biologicals) against the proposed **ICR (Integrated Campaign Registry) FHIR R4 IG** (IG v0.1.0 / explainer v0.7.0, publisher UNICEF, built by Ona + Crosscut). Page numbers cite the **printed page numbers of the RED guide** (the document's own pagination, ≈ PDF page − 3). **RED is explicitly a *routine* immunization (RI) microplanning strategy**, "a strategy to guide immunization programmes, not a separate initiative or programme" (p.73), using a "'bottom-up' approach… start[ing] with health facility (service delivery) microplans which… make the district microplan" (p.1). The ICR IG is a *campaign* model; the IG draws the campaign-vs-routine boundary via `record-origin`. So the central question here is **how RED's routine-RI microplanning, denominator, catchment-geography, outreach-strategy and monitoring-for-action methodology map onto the IG's campaign model, and which RED concepts are routine-only vs reusable by campaigns.**

---

## 1. Executive summary

RED is the **methodological ancestor of almost every microplanning concept the ICR IG operationalizes for campaigns**, even though RED itself is about routine service delivery, not campaigns. RED's "six RED tools" — the map, session plan, workplan, stock record, drop-out tracking system, and monitoring chart (p.2) — and its five operational components are, in FHIR terms, exactly the inputs to an **ICRCampaign** CarePlan (the microplan = `plan`), an **ICRTargetPopulation** Group (the denominator), an **ICRLocation** with operational geography (the catchment map), a coded **delivery-strategy** (RED's fixed / outreach / mobile), per-visit **ICRCampaignTask**s (RED's sessions), and the coverage **MeasureReports** (RED's monitoring chart). RED independently arrives at several ICR design invariants: delivery strategy as a first-class coded axis ("decide the appropriate delivery strategy (fixed, outreach or mobile)", p.70; mapped F/O/M on the operational map, p.14); denominators with explicit provenance and a "use the highest / most accurate source" rule ("Compare population figures from different sources, such as national census, head count, NGO estimates, polio, measles, and supplemental immunization activities (SIA) results, and discuss which one is most accurate", p.69); operational geography overlaid on the catchment/admin hierarchy ("operational map of your catchment area", p.14); and "monitoring and use of data for action" realized as a coverage-vs-dropout monitoring chart that triggers corrective action (p.29–30). The IG's spine fits RED's domain well.

The largest **conceptual mismatch is the routine-vs-campaign boundary itself**. RED's atomic delivery model is a *recurring routine session* (fixed weekly / outreach monthly, "a minimum four sessions per year", p.20) feeding *cumulative monthly coverage* against an *annual cohort denominator* (infants <1 yr ≈ 2–4% of total population, p.69; monthly target = annual/12, p.29). The IG's CarePlan/Task model is built for a *bounded campaign round*. RED's recurring-schedule semantics (session frequency per month, "same day every week / same date every month", p.26), its **cumulative monthly monitoring-chart with an ideal-progress diagonal and dropout %** (p.29–30), and its **birth-cohort / continuous-denominator** logic are routine-RI constructs the IG does not model — and arguably should not, except where a multi-round campaign reuses the session-frequency and cumulative-coverage ideas. This is the cleanest example of a RED concept that is routine-only and should stay outside the campaign spine.

The most important **genuine, campaign-relevant gaps** RED exposes are: (1) **defaulter / dropout tracking as a first-class workflow** — RED devotes an entire step (Step 8, p.32–33) plus a dedicated monitoring metric (drop-out number DO and drop-out rate DO%, p.10–11, 30) to identifying individuals who started but did not complete and chasing them; the IG has `missed-reason` but no *defaulter / partially-completed / overdue* disposition and no dropout-rate measure. (2) **The two-axis "access vs utilization" problem typology** (Table 2, p.13) — RED categorizes every area into 4 problem categories from DTP1 coverage (access) × dropout rate (utilization); the IG has no equivalent coded problem/diagnosis axis. (3) **Supportive supervision** as a named, monitored, budgeted activity (RED component 2; supervisory visits scheduled on the workplan and tracked, p.5, 56, 63) — no IG profile. (4) **Community linkage / engagement** as a named component with concrete coded activities (RED component 3, p.5, 24, 32) — no IG axis. (5) **Resource / logistics / cold-chain microplanning** — RED's stock record, budgeting checklist and cold-chain line items (p.34–36, 60–62) go well beyond the IG's SupplyDelivery delivery event.

Highest-value additions for the IG: a **defaulter / dropout axis** (missed-reason `defaulter`/`overdue`; a dropout-rate Measure); an **access-vs-utilization problem-category** value set; **special-population / vulnerability** codes (rural-remote, nomadic/mobile, urban-poor/slum, socio-economic/minority, conflict-affected — p.16, 47–49); **denominator-source** additions (`head-count`/`enumeration`, `sia-results`/`campaign-results`, `community-register`); a **session-frequency / recurring-schedule** representation if campaigns reuse RED outreach planning; and explicit **supervision, community-engagement, and cold-chain/logistics** axes (mostly as routine-RI context, but several are campaign-relevant). The bottom line: the IG's spine is **adequate and well-validated** for the *campaign* projection of RED, but RED surfaces a cluster of equity/defaulter/supervision/community concepts that campaigns also need and the IG does not yet code.

## 2. Where the document ALIGNS with the IG

**Microplanning as a versioned plan; bottom-up aggregation → plan/order.** RED is built bottom-up: health-facility microplans are "put together to make the district microplan" (p.1, 2, 7). The HF microplan (session plan + workplan, Tables 4 & 6) is a *plan*; the workplan with scheduled-vs-held dates (Table 6: "Date(s) scheduled / Date(s) held", p.28) is its execution. This maps cleanly to **ICRCampaign** as an umbrella CarePlan with per-area/per-facility child CarePlans (`partOf`), `plan` for the microplan and `order` at execution, and **ICRCampaignProtocol** (PlanDefinition) as the reusable template (RED itself is the template: "a framework to guide problem-solving", p.73). The district consolidates "all the health facility workplans into a calendar type format" (p.56) — exactly umbrella-CarePlan aggregation.

**One Task per session/visit; persons hang off the delivery events.** RED's atomic field unit is the **session** (fixed, outreach, or mobile), not the individual: "A session plan… details the number of sessions, and therefore days or visits, needed to serve the entire target population" (p.71). Workload is measured per session (≈80 injections/fixed, ≈40/outreach, p.20), session implementation is monitored as "Number of sessions held / Number of sessions planned" (p.28), and tally sheets are collated "from each session conducted" (p.50). This directly supports **ICRCampaignTask = one Task per visit/session**, with individual children recorded as **ICRImmunizationEvent** delivery events. RED reserves person-level chasing for the defaulter (Step 8, p.32) — matching the IG's person-focused Task being reserved for missed/overdue chasing.

**Delivery strategy is first-class and coded (fixed / outreach / mobile).** RED instructs planners to "decide the type of session suitable for each village/ward… (fixed, outreach, mobile teams)" and to mark the operational map "using the letters F (fixed), O (outreach), M (mobile teams)" (p.14, 54). The session-type definitions (p.15) — Fixed = at the HF; Outreach = "an 'outreach site'… the staff can visit in one day"; Mobile = "beyond the outreach area… requires overnight stay" — are a clean three-value coded axis carried through the session plan (Table 4 col IV), workplan, monthly report ("VACCINATION STRATEGIES: Fixed / Outreach", p.38) and budget. This validates ICR `delivery-strategy` (`fixed-post`, `mobile`, and — see §5 — an **outreach** value) and the Type A/B typology. **Note the divergence:** RED's *outreach* and *mobile* are not identical to the IG's `temporary-post`/`mobile`; "outreach session" is a distinct concept (a periodic fixed-location visit reachable in a day) the IG does not name (§5).

**Denominators with provenance, and a "compare sources / pick the most accurate" rule.** RED uses the infant <1-yr target population (Table 1 col b) and is explicit about provenance: "Compare population figures from different sources, such as national census, head count, NGO estimates, polio, measles, and supplemental immunization activities (SIA) results, and discuss which one is most accurate" (p.69); the infant target "may range from 2% to 4% of the total population" (p.69); for hard-to-reach areas, "Review results of previous polio or measles campaigns… meet the residents… and ask for their help in counting the number of families" (p.45); for urban high-risk areas, make "an operational estimate of infants… bearing in mind that official population figures may not include these areas at all" (p.48), using "existing immunization denominator estimates / results of recent immunization campaigns / household surveys / NGO information" (p.48). This strongly validates **ICRTargetPopulation** (Group actual=false) and the **denominator source + date** extension, and the `denominator-source` codes `census`, `census-projection`, `microcensus`, `hmis`, `other` — plus motivates additions (§5). RED also flags the **denominator problem** directly: "Is there a big difference between official denominator and immunization denominator?" (p.24) and "In the absence of accurate population estimates, the coverage and other 'rates' are likely to be exaggerated… or underestimated" (p.64) — the exact "no denominator without provenance" invariant.

**Catchment / operational geography overlaid on admin hierarchy, multi-source.** RED's Step 2 is "Preparing and reviewing an operational map" of "the area served by your HF catchment area" (p.14), marking villages/settlements, total + target population per village/ward, hard-to-reach areas, roads/landmarks, distances, nomadic movement, and seasonal accessibility (p.14). The district map shows "the entire catchment area of the district / each HF and its catchment population / priority areas" (p.54). Urban mapping goes street-by-street with housing type and colour-coded high-risk areas (p.47–48). This validates **ICRLocation** with admin hierarchy (`partOf`) **overlaid** by operational geography (the catchment/session map), plus location types `admin-unit`, `settlement`, `facility`, `community-distribution-point` (the outreach site), `temporary-post`, `operational-area`, `supervisory-area`. RED even reuses polio/measles campaign maps for RI ("there may already be good maps in use for polio eradication… decide how they can be modified for… routine immunization microplanning", p.70) — direct support for shared operational geography across campaign and routine.

**Monitoring-for-action vs coverage; administrative coverage formula.** RED's administrative coverage is explicit: coverage = doses administered ÷ target population × 100 (Table 1 cols g–j, p.10), plotted cumulatively on the monitoring chart against an "ideal rate of progress" diagonal (p.29). This maps to **ICRAdministrativeCoverage** (MeasureReport). RED also has an *independent validation* instrument — the convenient household questionnaire (Annex 1, p.39–41) — explicitly framed as a data-quality/validation check, "intended to supplement, not replace, routine reporting" and "a simple means of verifying data quality. If a health facility reports very high coverage, yet several unimmunized children are found, the data quality should be questioned" (p.40). This supports the IG's separation of **ICRAdministrativeCoverage** from **ICRSurveyCoverage** and the principle that independently-measured data is a separate lineage that must never be merged with reported coverage. (Caveat: RED's questionnaire is a *convenience* sample, explicitly "not representative of any population", p.39 — so it maps to a survey/independent lineage but **not** to a probability `survey` coverage estimate; see §3b.)

**Record-origin firewall (campaign vs routine) is RED's own framing.** RED repeatedly distinguishes routine sessions from "special campaigns / SIAs / NIDs" (p.26, 57, 62) and treats SIA results as a *separate* denominator source to be cross-compared, not merged ("the tally sheets and reports from SIAs often list the total number of children under five reached by village for each round", p.69). The budget separates "Routine recurrent cost" from "Campaigns operational costs (Polio / Measles / Yellow fever / MNT campaigns)" (Table 10, p.62) and even separates "Social mobilization and communication: For routine services | For campaigns" (Table 9, p.61). This is direct field-level endorsement of the IG's mandatory `record-origin` = `campaign` vs `routine` firewall.

**Realtime vs reconciled lineage (informal → formal, then verified).** RED's monthly report is the *formal* channel ("Every health facility is required to provide a report to the next level every month", p.37), and data quality is assured by reconciliation: "Have the number of 'tally marks' been correctly transferred to the summary form?… Check the tally sheets randomly… Are the data consistent? Check… the data at the health facility and district levels are the same" (p.64). This supports `data-lineage` = `realtime` vs `reconciled` through one structure, with reconciliation as a defined cross-check pass.

**Integration / other interventions delivered with immunization.** RED systematically plans "other child survival interventions… (e.g. insecticide treated bednets, deworming tablets, etc.)" delivered with immunization (Table 4 col IX, p.20; p.45, 72), with the integration decision "taken at national or sub-national level" but logistics planned locally (p.72). This validates the IG `campaign-type` codes `integrated`, `itn-distribution` (bednets), `vitamin-a` (the monthly report has a "VITAMIN A SUPPLEMENTATION" column, p.38) and MDA-style `mda` (deworming), and the separate delivery-event profiles (Immunization vs MedicationAdministration vs SupplyDelivery) keyed to a shared denominator.

## 3. Gaps & divergences

### 3a. Things the document requires that the IG does NOT yet represent

- **Defaulter / dropout tracking as a first-class workflow + metric (real gap, campaign-relevant).** RED dedicates Step 8 ("Working with the community and tracking defaulters", p.32–33) and a whole monitoring metric to people who *started but did not complete*: the **drop-out number DO = Penta1 − Penta3** and **drop-out rate DO% = (Penta1−Penta3)/Penta1 × 100** (Table 1 cols m–n, p.10–11; monitoring chart, p.30). It describes concrete tracking mechanisms — the immunization register review ("if an infant received Penta1 in February, check… Penta2 in March", p.32) and "reminder cards" filed by month-due (p.33) — and follow-up ("make sure that every infant receives the vaccinations that are overdue", p.33). The IG's `missed-reason` (`absent`/`sleeping`/`sick`/`refusal`/`inaccessible`/`not-visited`/`other`) covers *not reached at a visit*, but has **no `defaulter`/`overdue`/`partially-immunized` disposition** and **no dropout-rate Measure**. Multi-round/multi-dose campaigns (e.g. multi-round MDA, multi-dose vaccination SIAs) have the identical "completed dose 1 but not dose 2" problem, so this is genuinely campaign-relevant. (Mark: **real gap.**)

- **Access-vs-utilization problem typology (real gap).** RED Table 2 (p.13) categorizes every area on two axes — **access** (DTP1 coverage ≥80% good / <80% poor) and **utilization** (dropout ≤10% good / >10% poor) — into 4 problem categories (1 = no problem, 2 = good access/poor utilization, 3 = poor access/good utilization, 4 = poor access/poor utilization), used to prioritize areas and select corrective activities (Table 1 cols o–q, p.11; Table 3, p.18). The IG has **no coded problem/diagnosis axis** for an operational area. This is a structured, actionable classification campaigns also need (a campaign area that is "reached but defaulting" needs different action than "never reached"). (Mark: **real gap.**)

- **Special-population / equity / vulnerability typology (real gap).** RED Step 3 (p.16) classifies the hard-to-reach into a clear taxonomy: **rural hard-to-reach** (too far; seasonally mobile/nomadic), **urban hard-to-reach** (slum dwellers, squatter settlements), and **socio-economic hard-to-reach** (minority/religious/traditional, daily-wage earners, conflict/insecurity areas), plus **urban high-risk** ("slums", makeshift housing, no street names, homeless, transients, p.47–49). The IG has location types like `settlement` but **no population-vulnerability / equity axis** to flag *why* a delivery unit or area is hard-to-reach. Equity is central to campaign targeting, so this is campaign-relevant. (Mark: **real gap** — note IG acknowledges localization/ConceptMap but not an equity axis.)

- **Supportive supervision as a named, monitored, budgeted activity (real gap, partly routine).** Supervision is RED component 2 (p.5), with concrete monitored signals: "Date of last supervisory visit" on the monthly report (p.38), supervisory visits scheduled on the district calendar marked "S" (p.56), the district supervisory plan/schedule (p.44), per-diems "for supervision and monitoring" budgeted (p.62), and qualitative checks ("Do you receive regular supervisory visits? …how many in the last six months?", p.24). The IG has **no supervision/QA profile** (an Observation/Task or MeasureReport family). Supervision is equally a campaign activity. (Mark: **real gap.**)

- **Community linkage / engagement as a named component with coded activities (real gap, campaign-relevant).** Community links is RED component 3 (p.5), with discrete activities: meet community leaders to inform of session time/place, "record new births or pregnant women", motivate parents, "request the support of volunteers", and "request help in finding defaulters" (p.32); plus feedback gathering (Annex 1) and KABP-style awareness ("Are pregnant women aware of the need for TT/Td?", "Are there rumours and concerns about the safety of vaccines?", p.24). The IG has `noncompliance-reason` (`misinformation`, `religious-objection`, etc.) for refusals but **no community-engagement / demand / social-mobilization axis**. (Mark: **real gap.**)

- **Resource / logistics / cold-chain microplanning (real gap, partly acknowledged via SupplyDelivery).** RED's Step 9 stock record tracks per-vaccine **batch #, expiry, VVM status, doses received/issued, balance** (p.34–36); the monthly report tracks "Quantity received / Stock balance / Vaccine doses discarded due to VVM change / Temperature min–max" and "Total doses opened" (p.38); the budget itemizes **cold-chain running costs, refrigerators/cold boxes/vaccine carriers, fuel, safety boxes** (Tables 9 & 10, p.61–62); and corrective actions include "cold-chain rehabilitation" and "respond to stock out report" (p.65–66). The IG's **ICRSupplyDelivery** records a delivery event but does **not** model stock balance, VVM/temperature/cold-chain status, wastage/doses-opened, or a logistics-readiness axis. (Mark: **real gap**, beyond the SupplyDelivery delivery event.)

- **AEFI surveillance (real gap).** RED's monthly report includes a structured AEFI block: "Type of AEFI (Abscesses / Anaphylaxis / Other allergic reactions / BCG Lymphadenitis / Deaths)" × "Vaccines-related events (BCG/Polio/Penta/MSL/TT/YF/HepB)" with case counts (p.38), and short-term corrective action "investigate outbreaks and adverse events following immunization (AEFI)" (p.66). No ICR profile or value set covers AEFI. (Mark: **real gap** — consistent with the SIA-guide analysis.)

- **Disease-surveillance / VPD-case data feeding action (real gap).** The monthly report carries a "Synthesis of disease surveillance" block (AFP, Measles, MNT, Diphtheria, Pertussis, YF, Meningitis, Malaria) with cases/deaths by age band (p.38), and RED's Step 6 "Taking action" turns coverage × VPD-case patterns into corrective decisions (high coverage + high measles cases ⇒ pockets of unreached, p.65). The IG has no surveillance/Condition profile to close this monitoring-for-action loop. (Mark: **real gap**, largely routine-RI but relevant where campaigns are outbreak-response.)

- **Recurring-session schedule / session-frequency semantics (real gap, mostly routine-only).** RED plans *recurring* sessions: number and type of sessions per month (Table 4 cols VII–VIII), "fixed sessions on the same day(s) every week; outreach sessions on the same day or date every month" (p.26), "a minimum four sessions per year" (p.20). The IG's CarePlan/Task model represents discrete planned tasks, not a recurring schedule with a frequency. This is largely **routine-only**, but a multi-round campaign reusing RED outreach planning would need session-frequency/round-cadence representation (FHIR `Timing`). (Mark: **modelling gap**, mostly routine-RI.)

### 3b. Things the IG models that the document treats differently (or contradicts)

- **Denominator semantics: continuous birth-cohort vs bounded campaign target.** RED's denominator is a *continuous annual cohort* (infants <1 yr, monthly target = annual/12, p.29; pregnant women), reflecting routine RI's rolling eligibility. The IG's **ICRTargetPopulation** is a *bounded campaign-round* estimate. These are not contradictory but are *different temporal models*; the IG should be clear that ICRTargetPopulation is a snapshot denominator for a round, whereas RED's is a flow. (Mark: **modelling choice / routine-only nuance.**)

- **Coverage measurement: cumulative monthly chart vs round coverage.** RED's monitoring chart is **cumulative across 12 months against an ideal diagonal**, with dropout overlaid (p.29–30) — a time-series, not a single round coverage figure. The IG's **ICRAdministrativeCoverage** MeasureReport is naturally a per-round figure. The cumulative-progress + dropout-overlay chart is a routine construct; campaigns would express it as a series of MeasureReports. (Mark: **modelling choice**, routine-RI.)

- **"Outreach" is a distinct strategy, not the IG's mobile/temporary-post.** RED draws a 3-way distinction: **fixed** (at HF), **outreach** (a periodic visit to a site reachable and returnable in one day), **mobile** (beyond outreach, overnight) (p.15). The IG `delivery-strategy` has `fixed-post`, `temporary-post`, `mobile`, `house-to-house`, `community-directed`, `school` — but **no explicit `outreach` value**. RED's "outreach session at an outreach site" most closely resembles `temporary-post`/`community-distribution-point`, but the document treats *outreach* as its own first-class word throughout (monthly report column, budget line, session-plan divisor of 40 vs 80). (Mark: **terminology divergence** — see §5.)

- **Independent validation is a convenience sample, not a probability survey.** The IG's `coverage-source` includes `survey` (and `lqas`, `rcm`). RED's only independent instrument (Annex 1) is explicitly a **convenience** household questionnaire ("households do not have to be randomly selected", "results are not representative of any population other than the households you interview", p.39) — a data-quality check, not a coverage estimate. The IG should not map RED's questionnaire to `survey` (probability) coverage; it is closer to a `rcm`-style/quality-check lineage. (Mark: **modelling clarification** — IG `sample-design` extension should distinguish convenience checks from probability surveys.)

- **No house-to-house / community-directed in RED.** RED's strategies are fixed/outreach/mobile only; it has **no house-to-house or community-directed delivery** (those are campaign/MDA constructs). RED's defaulter follow-up *visits households* but for tracing individuals, not blanket house-to-house delivery. So `house-to-house` and `community-directed` (Type B/C) are correctly campaign-only and absent from RED. (Mark: **not a gap** — confirms IG codes that are campaign-specific.)

## 5. Proposed terminology additions (flag for the IG)

- **`delivery-strategy`: add `outreach`** (CodeSystem `delivery-strategy`). RED treats "outreach session at an outreach site" as a first-class strategy distinct from both fixed and mobile, with its own workload divisor (40 vs 80 injections), monthly-report column, and budget line (p.15, 20, 38, 70). Today an implementer must misclassify it as `temporary-post` or `mobile`. Justification: p.15 (definitions), p.70 (FAQ "decide the appropriate delivery strategy (fixed, outreach or mobile)").

- **`location-type`: add `outreach-site`** (CodeSystem `location-type`). The "outreach site… the staff can visit in one day" (p.15) is a recurring operational delivery point distinct from `temporary-post` (one-off) and `community-distribution-point`. Justification: p.14–15, 54.

- **`denominator-source`: add `head-count`/`enumeration`, `community-register`, `campaign-results`/`sia-results`.** RED explicitly derives denominators from local head-counts/family-counting (p.45, 48), community registers/immunization registers (p.64), and prior SIA/NID/polio/measles campaign results ("Dividing this total by five will give an approximate number for the infant population", p.69). Current `denominator-source` (`census`, `census-projection`, `microcensus`, `worldpop`, `grid3`, `hmis`, `other`) lacks head-count enumeration and campaign-results as named provenances. Justification: p.45, 48, 69.

- **`missed-reason` (or a new disposition axis): add `defaulter`/`overdue` and `partially-immunized`.** RED's central monitoring construct is the person who *started but did not complete* — "partially immunized", overdue for the next dose, the defaulter to be traced (p.32, 39). This is not "absent at a visit"; it is a longitudinal completion status. Justification: p.10–11 (DO/DO%), p.30, p.32–33, p.39.

- **New `population-vulnerability` / `special-population` value set** (CodeSystem to add): `rural-remote`, `nomadic`/`seasonally-mobile`, `urban-poor`/`slum`/`squatter`, `socio-economic-minority` (religious/traditional/daily-wage), `conflict-affected`/`insecurity`, `homeless`/`transient`. Justification: p.16 (3-way hard-to-reach taxonomy), p.47–49 (urban high-risk).

- **New `operational-problem-category` value set** (access × utilization): `category-1-no-problem`, `category-2-poor-utilization`, `category-3-poor-access`, `category-4-poor-access-and-utilization`, plus the two underlying coded axes `access` (good/poor by DTP1) and `utilization` (good/poor by dropout). Justification: Table 2, p.13; Table 1 cols o–q, p.11.

- **`coverage-source` / `sample-design`: distinguish convenience vs probability.** Add a `convenience`/`quality-check` value or a `sample-design` flag so RED's "not representative" household questionnaire (p.39) is not coded as a probability `survey`. Justification: p.39–40.

## 6. Categories / value sets worth adding

- **Dropout / continuation tracking axis (recommend ADD — campaign-relevant).** A defaulter/dropout disposition (§5) plus a **dropout-rate / series-continuation Measure** (DO# and DO%, p.10, 30). Multi-dose/multi-round campaigns share the completion problem. *Belongs in the IG.*

- **Access-vs-utilization problem-category axis (recommend ADD).** The 4-category problem typology (p.13) is a reusable operational diagnosis for any campaign area (reached-but-defaulting vs never-reached). *Belongs in the IG* (as a coded Observation/flag on operational area or MeasureReport).

- **Special-population / equity / vulnerability axis (recommend ADD — campaign-critical).** The hard-to-reach/underserved taxonomy (p.16, 47–49) directly drives campaign micro-targeting and equity reporting. *Belongs in the IG.*

- **Supportive-supervision / QA axis (recommend ADD, scoped).** Supervisory-visit records, schedules, and quality observations (p.5, 38, 56, 63). Campaigns also supervise. *Belongs in the IG* as a supervision profile (Task/Observation) + a small supervision-finding value set; much of the day-to-day routine cadence can stay out.

- **Community-engagement / demand / social-mobilization axis (recommend ADD, scoped).** Community-leader meetings, volunteer mobilization, birth/pregnancy notification, KABP/awareness, rumour/safety-concern tracking (p.5, 24, 32). *Belongs in the IG* (overlaps the SIA-guide's social-mobilization gap); coordinate with `noncompliance-reason`.

- **Cold-chain / logistics / resource-readiness axis (recommend ADD, scoped).** Stock balance, VVM status, temperature excursions, doses opened/discarded/wastage, cold-chain equipment and fuel readiness (p.34–36, 38, 61–62). *Belongs in the IG* as a commodity/cold-chain readiness extension or profile beyond ICRSupplyDelivery; some budgeting detail is out of scope.

- **Monitoring-for-action indicator set (recommend ADD as Measures).** RED's actionable indicators: session completeness (held/planned, p.28), reporting completeness/timeliness (Table 11, p.64), coverage-vs-ideal-progress, dropout%, denominator-discrepancy (official vs immunization, p.24). These align with the IG's already-acknowledged "Measure definitions aligned to WHO reporting minimums" gap — RED supplies concrete minimums. *Belongs in the IG.*

- **AEFI + disease-surveillance axes (recommend ADD, mostly routine/outbreak context).** AEFI types (p.38) and VPD-case surveillance (p.38, 65). *Belongs in the IG* for outbreak-response campaigns; otherwise routine-RI context.

## 7. Use cases not yet identified in the IG

- **Defaulter tracing / overdue-dose chasing.** Identify children who received an earlier dose but not the next-due dose (register review / reminder cards, p.32–33) and follow them up. FHIR: person-focused **ICRCampaignTask** (`task-origin = field-registered`) with a new `defaulter`/`overdue` disposition + a Task output linking the prior **ICRImmunizationEvent**; a continuation/dropout **Measure**.

- **Dropout-rate monitoring for action.** Compute DO# and DO% per area, plot cumulative coverage vs ideal, and trigger corrective action when below the line (p.29–30, 65). FHIR: a dropout **Measure** + **MeasureReport** distinct from `administrative` coverage; a corrective-action Task.

- **Catchment re-mapping & head-count enumeration feeding denominators.** Bottom-up family counting / community census in hard-to-reach and urban-poor areas where official figures exclude the population (p.45, 48); revise estimates for mobile populations. FHIR: **ICRTargetPopulation** refresh with `denominator-source = head-count`/`enumeration` + date provenance; **ICRLocation** operational-geography re-draw.

- **Access-vs-utilization area classification & prioritization.** Score each area on access × utilization, assign problem category and priority rank by number unimmunized (Tables 1–3, p.11–18). FHIR: coded Observation/flag on the operational-area **ICRLocation**/CarePlan; a prioritization Measure.

- **Outreach / mobile session planning by workload.** Convert target population × injections into sessions/month per site, choose fixed/outreach/mobile, schedule recurring sessions (Tables 4 & 6, p.22, 28). FHIR: **ICRCampaign** child CarePlans per site with `delivery-strategy`; Tasks per session; FHIR `Timing` for recurrence (note recurring-schedule gap, §3a).

- **Supportive-supervision visits.** Schedule and record supervisory visits (calendar "S", last-visit date, findings) tied to priority areas (p.38, 56, 63). FHIR: a supervision **Task**/Encounter + Observation profile; a supervision-coverage Measure ("% of posts assessed").

- **Community-link engagement & demand activities.** Community-leader meetings, volunteer mobilization, birth/pregnancy notification, awareness/rumour tracking (p.24, 32). FHIR: engagement **Task**/Encounter + community-engagement value set; couple with `noncompliance-reason`.

- **Cold-chain / logistics / stock-readiness planning.** Stock-balance tracking with VVM/temperature/wastage and equipment readiness; respond to stock-outs and do cold-chain rehabilitation (p.34–36, 38, 65–66). FHIR: a cold-chain/commodity-readiness extension or profile beyond **ICRSupplyDelivery**.

- **Data-quality reconciliation pass.** Tally-to-summary transfer checks, HF↔district consistency, denominator/numerator validation, completeness/timeliness tracking (p.64, Table 11). FHIR: `data-lineage = reconciled` MeasureReports + a reporting-completeness Measure.

## 8. Bottom line

For the **campaign projection of RED's microplanning domain, the ICR IG spine is adequate and well-validated**: RED's map → operational geography, denominator → ICRTargetPopulation-with-provenance, session plan/workplan → CarePlan plan/order, session → one-Task-per-visit, fixed/outreach/mobile → delivery-strategy, and monitoring chart → administrative-coverage MeasureReport all map cleanly, and RED independently endorses the record-origin firewall and the planned/delivered/measured lineage separation. But RED surfaces a coherent cluster of **equity-, defaulter-, supervision-, and community-oriented concepts that campaigns also need and the IG does not yet code.**

**Top 3–5 recommended IG changes:**
1. **Add a defaulter/dropout axis** — `defaulter`/`overdue`/`partially-immunized` dispositions + a dropout-rate/continuation **Measure** (p.10, 30, 32). *Campaign-relevant.*
2. **Add a special-population / equity-vulnerability value set** (rural-remote, nomadic, urban-poor/slum, socio-economic-minority, conflict-affected) (p.16, 47–49). *Campaign-critical.*
3. **Add an access-vs-utilization operational-problem-category axis** (Table 2, p.13). *Campaign-relevant.*
4. **Reconcile delivery-strategy/location-type with RED** — add `outreach` (strategy) and `outreach-site` (location), and add `head-count`/`enumeration`, `community-register`, `campaign-results` to `denominator-source` (p.15, 45, 48, 69). *Campaign-relevant.*
5. **Add scoped supervision, community-engagement, and cold-chain/logistics axes** + a monitoring-for-action **Measure** set (session completeness, reporting completeness/timeliness, denominator discrepancy) (p.5, 24, 38, 56, 63–64). *Mixed; partly campaign-relevant.*

**Routine-RI-only vs campaign-relevant (explicit):**
- **Routine-RI-only (keep outside the campaign spine):** the cumulative 12-month monitoring chart with ideal-progress diagonal (p.29); the continuous birth-cohort / monthly-target (annual÷12) denominator model (p.29); recurring fixed-weekly/outreach-monthly session-frequency scheduling (p.20, 26); the standing immunization register and reminder-card defaulter mechanics (p.32–33); the routine monthly-report form and routine budget cadence (p.37–38, 60–62).
- **Campaign-relevant (reusable, worth coding):** delivery-strategy (fixed/outreach/mobile) + operational map; denominator-with-provenance and the denominator-problem; the defaulter/dropout *concept* (multi-dose/multi-round completion); the access-vs-utilization problem typology; the special-population/equity taxonomy; supportive supervision; community engagement/demand; cold-chain/logistics readiness; and the record-origin routine-vs-campaign firewall (which RED itself enforces in its denominators, data, and budget).
